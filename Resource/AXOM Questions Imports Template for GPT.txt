Question 1: A 45-year-old woman comes to the office due to 3 days of progressive pain in the right upper quadrant, nausea and vomiting. In the preceding year, the patient had fatigue, weight loss, joint pain and intermittent abdominal pain. Lab studies show increased blood urea nitrogen and serum creatinine. Right upper quadrant ultrasound shows no evidence of gallstones but an inflamed gallbladder is present. The patient undergoes cholecystectomy and subsequent biopsy of the submucosal gallbladder vessels is shown in the image (medium-sized vessel with transmural necrotizing inflammation and fibrinoid change). Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Angiodysplasia
B. Antiphospholipid-antibody syndrome
C. Granulomatosis with polyangiitis
D. Polyarteritis nodosa
E. Thromboangiitis obliterans

Answer: D
Explanation: Systemic symptoms, renal injury, abdominal pain, and necrotizing inflammation of medium-sized gallbladder vessels indicate polyarteritis nodosa.
Review: Polyarteritis nodosa: medium-vessel pathology, organ distribution, and hepatitis B association.

Question 2: A 68-year-old man presents to his primary care physician for follow-up of poorly controlled hypertension. He reports occasional headaches but denies chest pain, shortness of breath, or visual changes. His medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus, stage 3 chronic kidney disease, and a prior myocardial infarction 5 years ago. His current medications include metformin, atorvastatin, and lisinopril. He states that he has been compliant with his medications and diet. His blood pressure is 162/94 mmHg and heart rate is 78/min. Physical examination is notable for mild bilateral lower extremity edema. No murmurs or pulmonary crackles are appreciated. Laboratory studies show serum sodium: 138 mEq/L; serum potassium: 5.9 mEq/L; serum creatinine: 2.3 mg/dL (baseline 2.0 mg/dL); BUN: 34 mg/dL; HbA1c: 7.8%. Which of the following medications is most contraindicated in this patient?

A. Metoprolol
B. Betaxolol
C. Furosemide
D. Amlodipine
E. Diltiazem
F. Spironolactone

Answer: F
Explanation: Spironolactone would further impair potassium excretion in a patient with CKD, ACE-inhibitor use, and potassium of 5.9 mEq/L, creating a dangerous hyperkalemia risk.
Review: Mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonists: potassium and renal contraindications.

Question 3: A 64-year-old man comes to the emergency department due to chest discomfort and shortness of breath. Medical history includes hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and severe osteoarthritis of the left knee. The patient is an active smoker with a 20-pack-year smoking history. He undergoes dobutamine infusion with simultaneous cardiac imaging. Before the infusion, there is normal left ventricular contractility, and an ejection fraction of 60%. During the infusion, decreased apical contractility and ejection fraction of 45% was seen. Five minutes after infusion, normal ventricular contractility and ejection fraction of 60% was observed. Which of the following best explains the observed findings?

A. Coronary artery occlusion due to plaque thrombosis
B. Coronary artery vasospasm
C. Focal myocardial fibrosis
D. Normal myocardial contractile response
E. Supply-demand mismatch due to fixed coronary stenosis

Answer: E
Explanation: Dobutamine raises heart rate and contractility, increasing oxygen demand. Reversible regional hypokinesis during stress indicates inducible ischemia from a fixed coronary stenosis.
Review: Dobutamine stress testing and myocardial supply-demand mismatch.

Question 7: A 60-year-old man comes to the office for a routine follow-up visit. He feels well overall except for an intermittent, mild, generalized headache. The patient has no known medical problems and takes no medications. He does not smoke, follows a healthy diet and exercises daily. On examination, his BP is 150/85 mmHg and he is started on lisinopril. At a follow-up visit, his BP is 128/78 mmHg. He also has a dry cough that began a few weeks after starting lisinopril. This drug is stopped and losartan is now prescribed. The patient seems to be compliant with his medication, the cough resolves and he experiences no other side effects. When compared with no treatment, this patient’s current therapy is most likely to result in which of the following changes? Renin Angiotensin I Angiotensin II Aldosterone Bradykinin

A. Renin ↓; Angiotensin I ↓; Angiotensin II ↓; Aldosterone ↓; Bradykinin ↔
B. Renin ↑; Angiotensin I ↓; Angiotensin II ↓; Aldosterone ↓; Bradykinin ↔
C. Renin ↑; Angiotensin I ↑; Angiotensin II ↓; Aldosterone ↓; Bradykinin ↑
D. Renin ↑; Angiotensin I ↑; Angiotensin II ↑; Aldosterone ↓; Bradykinin ↔
E. Renin ↑; Angiotensin I ↑; Angiotensin II ↑; Aldosterone ↑; Bradykinin ↔

Answer: D
Explanation: Losartan blocks AT1 receptors, removing feedback inhibition so renin, angiotensin I, and angiotensin II rise; aldosterone falls and bradykinin remains unchanged.
Review: RAAS laboratory changes with ARBs versus ACE inhibitors.

Over the last 3 months, the patient has been hospitalized twice due to acute decompensation of heart failure that required treatment with intravenous furosemide. Serum creatinine is 1.2 mg/dL and serum potassium is 4.1 mEq/L. The patient is started on metolazone. The addition of metolazone is likely to assist in treating this patient because of which of the following effects of furosemide?

A. Increased calcium delivery to the distal tubules
B. Increased sodium delivery to the distal tubules
C. Increased tubular reabsorption to bicarbonate
D. Increase tubular reabsorption of uric acid
E. Reduced tubular reabsorption of glucose
