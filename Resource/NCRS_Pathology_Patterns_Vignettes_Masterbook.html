1.01 Vessel-wall injury and arteriosclerosis

10 cards · source p. 3
Embolism
High yieldp. 3
Definition

Intravascular material travels from its origin and lodges downstream, obstructing flow.

Pattern

Most are thromboemboli; other forms include fat, air, amniotic fluid, septic, tumor, and cholesterol emboli. Clinical effect depends on material, size, and vascular bed.

Vignette

A patient with a recent proximal DVT develops abrupt dyspnea, pleuritic pain, tachycardia, and hypoxemia.

Distinguish

Thrombosis forms in place; embolism travels from another site.

Outcome

Pulmonary embolism, systemic infarction, paradoxical embolism, or sudden death.

Thrombosis
High yieldp. 3
Definition

Formation of a solid intravascular mass from blood constituents during life.

Pattern

Virchow triad: endothelial injury, abnormal flow, and hypercoagulability. Arterial thrombi are platelet rich and often occlusive; venous thrombi are red-cell/fibrin rich and propagate toward the heart.

Vignette

A hospitalized patient with cancer and prolonged immobility develops unilateral leg swelling; ultrasound shows a noncompressible deep vein.

Distinguish

A postmortem clot is gelatinous and nonadherent; a true thrombus is attached and may show lines of Zahn.

Outcome

Embolization, infarction, organization, and recanalization.

Atheroembolism
p. 3
Definition

Embolization of cholesterol crystal debris from an ulcerated atherosclerotic plaque into distal arterioles.

Pattern

Occurs after plaque disruption or vascular manipulation; produces blue toe, livedo reticularis, renal injury, and needle-shaped cholesterol clefts in small vessels.

Vignette

Three days after coronary angiography, a 72-year-old man develops livedo reticularis, painful blue toes despite palpable pedal pulses, eosinophilia, and a rising creatinine.

Atherosclerosis
p. 3
Definition

Chronic intimal disease of large and medium arteries driven by lipid retention, endothelial injury, inflammation, and smooth-muscle matrix production.

Pattern

Fatty streak progresses to fibrofatty plaque. A stable plaque has a thick cap; a vulnerable plaque has a thin cap, large lipid core, and inflammation, predisposing to rupture, thrombosis, embolism, aneurysm, or ischemic infarction.

Vignette

A 67-year-old man with a 45-pack-year smoking history develops exertional calf pain and a carotid bruit. An excised arterial lesion has a fibrous cap over a necrotic lipid core containing foam cells and cholesterol clefts.

Distinguish

Do not confuse with arteriolosclerosis, which primarily affects small arteries and arterioles.

Fibromuscular intimal hyperplasia
p. 3
Definition

Nonatherosclerotic intimal thickening caused by smooth-muscle proliferation and extracellular matrix deposition.

Pattern

Appears after vascular injury or intervention and contributes to restenosis of grafts, stents, or repaired vessels.

Vignette

Six months after angioplasty and stent placement, a patient again develops exertional ischemic symptoms. The treated segment is narrowed by smooth-muscle proliferation and extracellular matrix beneath the endothelium.

Hyaline arteriolosclerosis
p. 3
Definition

Homogeneous pink thickening of arteriolar walls from plasma-protein leakage and increased extracellular matrix.

Pattern

Seen with long-standing benign hypertension and diabetes; narrows the lumen and causes chronic ischemia, including benign nephrosclerosis.

Vignette

A 61-year-old woman with long-standing mild hypertension and diabetes has slowly progressive renal insufficiency. Renal biopsy shows homogeneous eosinophilic thickening of arteriolar walls with luminal narrowing.

Hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis
p. 3
Definition

Concentric smooth-muscle and basement-membrane proliferation in arterioles.

Pattern

Onion-skin narrowing is associated with severe or malignant hypertension; fibrinoid necrosis may accompany acute vascular injury.

Vignette

A 44-year-old man presents with BP 242/138 mmHg, blurred vision, confusion, and acute kidney injury. Renal arterioles show concentric onion-skin narrowing with foci of fibrinoid necrosis.

Ischemia and infarction
p. 3
Definition

Ischemia is inadequate perfusion; infarction is tissue necrosis caused by prolonged ischemia or venous outflow obstruction.

Pattern

White infarcts occur in solid end-arterial organs; red infarcts occur with venous occlusion, loose tissues, dual blood supply, or reperfusion.

Vignette

An older patient with atrial fibrillation develops sudden flank pain and hematuria; imaging shows a wedge-shaped renal defect.

Distinguish

Ischemia can be reversible; infarction implies irreversible necrosis.

Outcome

Organ dysfunction, inflammation, healing by scar, or liquefaction in brain.

Monckeberg medial calcific sclerosis
p. 3
Definition

Calcification of the media of muscular arteries, usually without important luminal narrowing.

Pattern

Often an incidental radiographic finding in older adults; vessels may feel rigid but distal perfusion is usually preserved.

Vignette

A 76-year-old asymptomatic man has pipe-stem calcification of muscular arteries on a leg radiograph. Distal pulses and perfusion are preserved because the lumen is not significantly narrowed.

Vasospasm
p. 3
Definition

Transient pathologic constriction of vascular smooth muscle that reduces downstream blood flow without a fixed occlusion.

Pattern

Episodic ischemia with reversible narrowing; coronary vasospasm produces rest pain with transient ST elevation, while digital vasospasm produces color change.

Vignette

A patient has recurrent rest pain with transient ST elevation that resolves after a vasodilator; angiography between attacks shows no fixed critical stenosis.

Distinguish

Reversibility and episodic narrowing distinguish spasm from fixed atherosclerotic stenosis.

Outcome

Prolonged spasm can cause infarction or malignant arrhythmia.

1.02 Systemic and pulmonary hypertension

6 cards · source p. 3
Benign hypertension
p. 3
Definition

Chronic, usually moderate elevation of blood pressure without acute target-organ failure.

Pattern

Hyaline arteriolosclerosis, LV hypertrophy, retinal arteriolar narrowing, and slowly progressive renal injury develop over years.

Vignette

A patient with years of poorly controlled blood pressure has an S4, concentric LV hypertrophy, mild proteinuria, and finely granular kidneys.

Distinguish

Malignant hypertension has abrupt severe pressure plus acute organ injury, papilledema, fibrinoid necrosis, and onion-skin arterioles.

Outcome

Stroke, ischemic heart disease, heart failure, CKD, and aortic disease.

Cor pulmonale
p. 3
Definition

Right-ventricular hypertrophy and/or failure caused by pulmonary hypertension from lung or pulmonary vascular disease.

Pattern

Peripheral edema, raised JVP, hepatomegaly, and a right-sided heave occur after chronic pulmonary pressure overload.

Vignette

A 69-year-old man with severe COPD develops ankle edema, elevated JVP, hepatomegaly, and a parasternal heave. Echocardiography shows right-ventricular hypertrophy and dysfunction without primary left-sided disease.

Malignant hypertension
p. 3
Definition

Abrupt, severe hypertension with acute target-organ damage.

Pattern

Headache, visual change, neurologic deficit, chest pain, pulmonary edema, or AKI may accompany retinal hemorrhage/papilledema, hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis, and fibrinoid necrosis.

Vignette

A patient with BP 238/142 mmHg has severe headache, papilledema, retinal hemorrhages, hematuria, and rapidly rising creatinine. Arterioles demonstrate acute fibrinoid injury and concentric wall thickening.

Primary hypertension
p. 4
Definition

Persistent systemic arterial hypertension without one identifiable secondary cause.

Pattern

Usually asymptomatic for years, then causes LV hypertrophy, nephrosclerosis, retinal injury, accelerated atherosclerosis, heart failure, stroke, and aortic disease.

Vignette

Repeated office and home measurements in a 52-year-old patient show BP near 155/95 mmHg, but renal, endocrine, medication, and sleep evaluations reveal no single cause. Years later, ECG shows left-ventricular hypertrophy and urine testing shows low-grade albuminuria.

Pulmonary hypertension
p. 4
Definition

Elevated pulmonary arterial pressure from pulmonary vascular, left-heart, lung/hypoxic, thromboembolic, or multifactorial causes.

Pattern

Progressive exertional dyspnea, loud P2, RV heave, and eventual right-heart failure. Plexiform lesions are a classic severe arterial pattern.

Vignette

A 38-year-old woman has progressive exertional dyspnea, syncope, a loud P2, and a right-ventricular heave. Right-heart catheterization confirms markedly elevated pulmonary arterial pressure, and biopsy shows plexiform vascular lesions.

Secondary hypertension
p. 4
Definition

Hypertension caused by a defined renal, endocrine, vascular, medication, sleep, or pregnancy-related process.

Pattern

Suspect with abrupt onset, resistant disease, very young age, hypokalemia, renal bruit, episodic symptoms, or rapid deterioration.

Vignette

A 29-year-old woman develops abrupt, treatment-resistant hypertension with an abdominal bruit and worsening creatinine after starting an ACE inhibitor. The presentation prompts evaluation for a correctable underlying process.

1.03 Aneurysmal dilation, infection, and wall disruption

7 cards · source p. 4
Aortic dissection
High yieldp. 4
Definition

Blood enters a tear in the aortic intima and tracks through the media, creating a false channel.

Pattern

Abrupt tearing chest or back pain, pulse/BP asymmetry, new aortic regurgitation, or neurologic/renal ischemia. Proximal dissections threaten tamponade and require urgent surgical evaluation.

Vignette

A 63-year-old man with poorly controlled hypertension develops abrupt tearing chest pain radiating between the shoulder blades, unequal arm pressures, and a new diastolic murmur. Imaging shows an intimal flap extending through the ascending aorta.

Distinguish

Hypertension is the major acquired risk; Marfan and other medial disorders matter in younger patients.

Marfan-associated aortopathy
High yieldp. 4
Definition

Fibrillin-1 disorder weakens elastic tissue and predisposes to cystic medial degeneration of the ascending aorta.

Pattern

Tall habitus, lens dislocation, skeletal features, aortic-root dilation, aortic regurgitation, aneurysm, or dissection in a young person.

Vignette

A tall young adult with pectus deformity and lens dislocation develops tearing chest pain and a new diastolic murmur.

Distinguish

Syphilitic aneurysm reflects vasa-vasorum endarteritis and tree-bark scarring; Marfan disease reflects connective-tissue weakness.

Outcome

Aortic dissection, rupture, and severe aortic regurgitation.

Abdominal aortic aneurysm
p. 4
Definition

Atherosclerosis-associated dilation of the abdominal aorta, usually below the renal arteries.

Pattern

Often pulsatile and asymptomatic until pain or rupture. Risk rises with smoking, age, male sex, family history, and size; rupture causes sudden abdominal/back pain, hypotension, and shock.

Vignette

A 74-year-old male smoker with diffuse atherosclerosis develops sudden abdominal and back pain, hypotension, and a tender pulsatile abdominal mass. Imaging shows a large infrarenal aortic dilation with mural thrombus.

Berry aneurysm
p. 4
Definition

Saccular intracranial aneurysm at an arterial branch point in the circle of Willis.

Pattern

Rupture causes sudden thunderclap headache and subarachnoid hemorrhage; associations include hypertension, smoking, ADPKD, and connective-tissue weakness.

Vignette

A 46-year-old woman suddenly develops the worst headache of her life, vomiting, neck stiffness, and brief loss of consciousness. CT shows blood in the basal cisterns near a circle-of-Willis branch point.

Mycotic aneurysm
p. 4
Definition

Infection of an arterial wall that weakens it and causes aneurysmal dilation.

Pattern

Consider with bacteremia/endocarditis, fever, pain, and a rapidly changing aneurysm; rupture and sepsis are major complications.

Vignette

A patient with persistent bacteremia and infective endocarditis develops focal abdominal pain and a rapidly enlarging irregular arterial outpouching on serial imaging. Fever continues despite hemodynamic stability, raising concern for infected vessel-wall destruction.

Thoracic/syphilitic aneurysm
p. 4
Definition

Thoracic aortic dilation may reflect hypertension, connective-tissue disease, bicuspid valve disease, or tertiary syphilitic aortitis.

Pattern

Syphilitic endarteritis weakens the ascending aortic media, causing tree-bark scarring, aortic-root dilation, and aortic regurgitation.

Vignette

An older adult with untreated remote sexually transmitted infection develops hoarseness, widened mediastinum, bounding pulses, and a diastolic murmur. The ascending aorta is dilated and has wrinkled tree-bark intimal scarring.

True versus false aneurysm
p. 4
Definition

A true aneurysm contains all vessel-wall layers; a false aneurysm is an extravascular hematoma that communicates with the lumen.

Pattern

Describe shape as saccular or fusiform. Complications include rupture, thrombosis, embolization, compression, and impaired branch-vessel flow.

Vignette

Several weeks after femoral arterial catheterization, a patient notices a pulsatile groin mass with a bruit. Ultrasound shows blood flowing through a narrow neck into a sac contained by surrounding soft tissue rather than all layers of the arterial wall.

1.04 Large-vessel vasculitis

2 cards · source p. 4
Giant-cell arteritis
p. 4
Definition

Granulomatous inflammation of large and medium arteries, especially branches of the carotid, in older adults.

Pattern

New temporal headache, scalp tenderness, jaw claudication, elevated inflammatory markers, and risk of irreversible visual loss. Treat promptly when suspected.

Vignette

A 74-year-old woman has a new unilateral temporal headache, scalp tenderness, jaw fatigue while chewing, an ESR of 96 mm/hr, and transient monocular visual loss. The temporal artery is tender with a reduced pulse.

Takayasu arteritis
p. 4
Definition

Granulomatous large-vessel vasculitis of the aorta and major branches, usually in younger women.

Pattern

Weak or unequal pulses, limb claudication, BP discrepancy, bruits, and ocular or neurologic ischemia create the pulseless-disease pattern.

Vignette

A 24-year-old woman reports fever, fatigue, and arm pain with use; her left radial pulse is faint and systolic pressure differs by 35 mmHg between arms. A bruit is heard over the subclavian artery.

1.05 Medium- and distal-vessel inflammatory disease

4 cards · source p. 5
Buerger disease
p. 5
Definition

Segmental thrombosing inflammation of small and medium arteries and veins strongly linked to tobacco exposure.

Pattern

Young smoker with distal ischemia, claudication, ulcers/gangrene, Raynaud phenomenon, or migratory thrombophlebitis. Tobacco cessation is decisive.

Vignette

A 36-year-old heavy cigarette smoker has distal foot claudication, painful ischemic toe ulcers, Raynaud symptoms, and episodes of migratory superficial thrombophlebitis. Angiography shows segmental distal arterial occlusions.

Infectious vasculitis
p. 5
Definition

Direct microbial invasion or septic embolization injures a vessel wall and may cause thrombosis or aneurysm.

Pattern

Fever/bacteremia plus focal ischemia, hemorrhage, or a rapidly changing aneurysm; endocarditis is an important source.

Vignette

A febrile patient with positive blood cultures develops a painful enlarging arterial aneurysm and distal embolic lesions.

Distinguish

Systemic inflammatory vasculitis lacks direct organism invasion; cultures and source identification are decisive.

Outcome

Rupture, sepsis, thrombosis, and infarction.

Kawasaki disease
p. 5
Definition

Acute medium-vessel vasculitis of childhood with mucocutaneous and lymph-node inflammation.

Pattern

Prolonged fever plus conjunctival, oral, hand/foot, rash, and cervical-node findings; coronary aneurysm and thrombosis are the feared outcomes.

Vignette

A 3-year-old has six days of fever, bilateral nonpurulent conjunctivitis, a strawberry tongue, cervical lymphadenopathy, rash, and swollen red hands and feet. Echocardiography is obtained to assess a feared coronary complication.

Polyarteritis nodosa
p. 5
Definition

Transmural necrotizing inflammation of medium and small muscular arteries, classically sparing pulmonary arteries and glomeruli.

Pattern

Renal and mesenteric ischemia, hypertension, abdominal pain, neuropathy, skin nodules/livedo, and microaneurysms; may associate with hepatitis B.

Vignette

A middle-aged man with hepatitis B develops abdominal pain after meals, hypertension, tender skin nodules, livedo reticularis, and asymmetric foot drop. Angiography shows renal and mesenteric microaneurysms, while the lungs and glomeruli are spared.

1.06 Small-vessel ANCA-associated vasculitis

4 cards · source p. 5
Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis
High yieldp. 5
Definition

Eosinophil-rich granulomatous small-vessel vasculitis occurring in patients with asthma and allergic disease.

Pattern

Adult-onset asthma, eosinophilia, sinus disease, pulmonary infiltrates, neuropathy, skin lesions, and possible MPO-ANCA.

Vignette

A patient with years of asthma develops eosinophilia, migratory lung infiltrates, palpable purpura, and foot drop.

Distinguish

Asthma and eosinophilia distinguish EGPA from GPA and microscopic polyangiitis.

Outcome

Cardiac involvement, neuropathy, pulmonary hemorrhage, and renal disease.

Granulomatosis with polyangiitis
High yieldp. 5
Definition

Necrotizing granulomatous inflammation of respiratory tract with pauci-immune small-vessel vasculitis and glomerulonephritis.

Pattern

Chronic sinus/ear disease, pulmonary nodules or hemorrhage, and rapidly progressive renal failure; PR3-ANCA/c-ANCA is a classic association.

Vignette

An adult with recurrent sinusitis, hemoptysis, cavitary lung nodules, hematuria, and RBC casts deteriorates over days.

Distinguish

Microscopic polyangiitis lacks granulomatous upper-airway disease; anti-GBM disease shows linear immunofluorescence.

Outcome

Pulmonary hemorrhage and crescentic glomerulonephritis.

Microscopic polyangiitis
High yieldp. 5
Definition

Pauci-immune necrotizing small-vessel vasculitis without granulomatous inflammation.

Pattern

Pulmonary capillaritis, rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis, skin purpura, neuropathy, and MPO-ANCA/p-ANCA association.

Vignette

A patient has palpable purpura, hemoptysis, rising creatinine, and pauci-immune crescentic GN but no chronic sinus or granulomatous disease.

Distinguish

Absence of granulomas and upper-airway disease separates it from GPA.

Outcome

Pulmonary-renal failure and ischemic organ injury.

Immune-complex small-vessel vasculitis
p. 5
Definition

Vessel-wall injury caused by deposited immune complexes with complement activation and leukocytoclasia.

Pattern

Palpable purpura, arthralgia, abdominal or renal involvement, low complement in some causes, and granular immunoglobulin/complement deposition.

Vignette

A patient develops palpable purpura and arthralgia after infection or medication exposure; biopsy shows leukocytoclastic vasculitis with granular deposits.

Distinguish

Pauci-immune ANCA vasculitis has little or no immune deposition.

Outcome

Glomerulonephritis, bowel ischemia, or systemic organ involvement.

1.07 Chronic and acute limb ischemia

3 cards · source p. 5
Acute limb ischemia
p. 5
Definition

Sudden critical reduction in limb perfusion from embolus, thrombosis, dissection, or trauma.

Pattern

The six Ps: pain, pallor, pulselessness, paresthesia, paralysis, and poikilothermia. Neurologic loss indicates a threatened limb.

Vignette

A patient with atrial fibrillation develops sudden severe leg pain, pallor, absent pulses, paresthesia, and a cool foot. New weakness appears during evaluation, indicating an immediately threatened extremity.

Arterial versus venous ulcer
p. 5
Definition

Arterial ulcers reflect ischemia; venous ulcers reflect chronic venous hypertension.

Pattern

Arterial: distal, punched out, painful, cool limb, weak pulses. Venous: gaiter area, shallow irregular, edematous warm limb, pigmentation, pulses usually present.

Vignette

One patient has a painful punched-out toe ulcer, cool shiny skin, and absent pedal pulses; another has a shallow irregular medial-ankle ulcer with edema, warmth, pigmentation, and preserved pulses. The location and surrounding limb findings identify the underlying flow problem.

Peripheral arterial disease
p. 5
Definition

Atherosclerotic narrowing of limb arteries that limits blood flow, especially during exertion.

Pattern

Intermittent claudication, cool hairless skin, weak pulses, delayed capillary refill, dependent rubor, and distal punched-out ulcers. ABI is typically reduced.

Vignette

A 68-year-old smoker develops reproducible calf pain after walking two blocks that resolves with rest. The leg is cool and hairless with weak pedal pulses, dependent rubor, and an ankle-brachial index of 0.62.

1.08 Venous thrombosis and chronic venous hypertension

4 cards · source p. 6
Chronic venous insufficiency
p. 6
Definition

Failure of venous valves or persistent outflow obstruction causing sustained venous hypertension.

Pattern

Dependent edema, aching, varicosities, stasis dermatitis, hemosiderin pigmentation, lipodermatosclerosis, and shallow irregular ulcers near the medial malleolus.

Vignette

A patient who stands all day has chronic ankle edema, aching, brown hemosiderin pigmentation, and a shallow irregular ulcer above the medial malleolus. Pulses remain palpable and elevation improves the swelling.

Deep-vein thrombosis
p. 6
Definition

Thrombus in the deep venous system, promoted by stasis, endothelial injury, and hypercoagulability.

Pattern

Often unilateral swelling, pain, warmth, and tenderness, but examination is unreliable. Major danger is pulmonary embolism; use pretest probability plus D-dimer/imaging.

Vignette

Five days after hip surgery, a patient develops unilateral calf swelling, warmth, and tenderness. Compression ultrasound shows a noncompressible popliteal vein, and the major feared complication is migration to the pulmonary circulation.

Superficial thrombophlebitis
p. 6
Definition

Inflammation and thrombosis of a superficial vein.

Pattern

Tender, erythematous, palpable cord with more localized symptoms than DVT; migratory disease can signal systemic hypercoagulability or malignancy.

Vignette

A woman with varicose veins develops a localized tender erythematous cord along the medial calf without diffuse limb edema. The inflamed structure is palpable immediately beneath the skin.

Varicose veins
p. 6
Definition

Dilated, tortuous superficial veins produced by valvular incompetence and elevated venous pressure.

Pattern

Worsen with standing and improve with elevation; may lead to edema, skin change, thrombophlebitis, or ulceration.

Vignette

A multiparous woman has dilated tortuous superficial leg veins that worsen after prolonged standing and improve with elevation. She reports heaviness and itching but has preserved distal pulses.

1.09 Edema and painful-leg mimics

3 cards · source p. 6
Cellulitis
p. 6
Definition

Acute bacterial infection of dermis and subcutaneous tissue.

Pattern

Painful, warm, erythematous, poorly demarcated skin with possible fever; can mimic DVT but does not explain absent arterial pulses.

Vignette

A febrile patient has a rapidly expanding, painful, warm, poorly demarcated erythematous area on one lower leg after a skin fissure. Pulses are intact and no palpable venous cord is present.

Lymphedema
p. 6
Definition

Protein-rich interstitial swelling from impaired lymphatic drainage.

Pattern

Usually chronic, asymmetric, and eventually nonpitting with skin thickening. Primary causes reflect developmental abnormalities; secondary causes include surgery, radiation, tumor, infection, and obstruction.

Vignette

Years after axillary lymph-node dissection and radiation, a woman develops slowly progressive unilateral arm enlargement. The swelling becomes firm and nonpitting with thickened peau d'orange skin.

Ruptured Baker cyst
p. 6
Definition

Leakage of synovial fluid from a popliteal cyst into the calf.

Pattern

Acute calf pain and swelling can mimic DVT; ultrasound helps distinguish the two.

Vignette

A patient with knee osteoarthritis and a history of posterior knee fullness develops sudden calf pain and swelling. Venous duplex shows no thrombus but reveals fluid tracking from the popliteal space into the calf.

2.01 Coronary ischemia without infarction

3 cards · source p. 6
Prinzmetal angina
p. 6
Definition

Episodic coronary vasospasm causing transient transmural ischemia.

Pattern

Rest pain with transient ST elevation; responds to nitrates and calcium-channel blockers rather than a beta-blocker strategy.

Vignette

A 43-year-old smoker has episodes of chest pain before dawn with transient ST elevation that resolves after nitroglycerin. Angiography between attacks shows no critical fixed obstruction.

Stable angina
p. 6
Definition

Predictable transient myocardial ischemia from a fixed coronary stenosis without acute plaque rupture.

Pattern

Exertional pressure relieved by rest or nitroglycerin; biomarkers stay negative and resting ECG may be normal between episodes.

Vignette

A 59-year-old man reports substernal pressure whenever he walks uphill; it resolves within minutes of rest or nitroglycerin and has been unchanged for six months. Serial troponins are negative.

Unstable angina
p. 6
Definition

Ischemic chest pain from acute plaque change and partial thrombosis without detectable myocardial necrosis.

Pattern

New, worsening, or rest pain. Treat as acute coronary syndrome; biomarkers distinguish it from NSTEMI.

Vignette

A 64-year-old woman with known coronary disease develops increasingly frequent chest pressure, now occurring at rest. ECG shows transient ischemic changes, but serial high-sensitivity troponins remain below the infarction threshold.

2.02 Acute myocardial infarction and localization

3 cards · source p. 6
MI territory pattern
p. 6
Definition

The affected coronary artery predicts the wall, leads, conduction risk, and mechanical complication.

Pattern

LAD: anterior/septal; RCA: inferior and often AV node/RV; LCX: lateral/posterior. Match symptoms, serial ECGs, and troponin rather than one clue alone.

Vignette

A patient with acute chest pain has ST elevation in leads II, III, and aVF, bradycardia, elevated JVP, and clear lungs. The ECG and right-sided hemodynamics point to the culprit coronary territory.

NSTEMI
p. 6
Definition

Acute myocardial necrosis usually caused by subtotal coronary obstruction and subendocardial ischemia.

Pattern

Ischemic symptoms plus troponin elevation; ECG often shows ST depression or T-wave inversion rather than persistent ST elevation.

Vignette

A 70-year-old patient has prolonged crushing chest pressure, diaphoresis, ST depressions in the lateral leads, and a rising troponin concentration. Angiography reveals severe but incomplete coronary occlusion.

STEMI
p. 6
Definition

Acute transmural myocardial infarction usually caused by complete coronary occlusion.

Pattern

Persistent regional ST elevation with reciprocal changes and rising troponin; requires immediate reperfusion assessment.

Vignette

A 58-year-old man has 90 minutes of crushing substernal pain, diaphoresis, persistent regional ST elevation with reciprocal changes, and rising troponin. Emergent angiography demonstrates complete coronary occlusion.

2.03 Infarct evolution, reperfusion, and complications

5 cards · source p. 7
Post-MI mechanical complications
High yieldp. 7
Definition

Structural failure of infarcted myocardium during healing.

Pattern

Papillary-muscle rupture causes acute MR; septal rupture causes a new harsh murmur and shunt; free-wall rupture causes hemopericardium/tamponade; aneurysm causes HF, arrhythmia, or mural thrombus.

Vignette

On day 4 after an acute coronary event, a patient suddenly develops hypotension, flash pulmonary edema, and a new apical systolic murmur from a flail leaflet. A new harsh sternal murmur would suggest septal failure, whereas abrupt tamponade with electromechanical dissociation would suggest free-wall failure.

Chronic ischemic heart disease
p. 7
Definition

Progressive heart failure caused by cumulative ischemic myocardial damage and scarring.

Pattern

Cardiomegaly, patchy fibrosis, prior infarcts, ventricular dilation, arrhythmia, and low-output symptoms dominate.

Vignette

A man with several remote coronary events develops progressive exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, ventricular dilation, and a low ejection fraction. The heart contains patchy fibrosis and multiple healed infarcts.

MI morphologic time course
p. 7
Definition

Infarcted myocardium evolves from coagulative necrosis through inflammation and granulation tissue to scar.

Pattern

Early arrhythmia risk; neutrophils dominate over the first days; macrophage digestion creates maximal rupture risk around days 3-7; collagen scar later supports chronic remodeling.

Vignette

Four days after a prolonged episode of crushing chest pain, a patient dies suddenly. The affected myocardium is yellow-tan with a hyperemic border and contains macrophages digesting necrotic fibers, leaving the wall temporarily weak.

Reperfusion injury
p. 7
Definition

Additional injury that occurs when blood flow returns to ischemic myocardium.

Pattern

ROS, calcium overload, inflammation, contraction-band necrosis, hemorrhage, and reperfusion arrhythmias may appear despite salvaged tissue.

Vignette

Soon after an occluded coronary artery is opened by PCI, a patient develops a ventricular arrhythmia despite restored flow. Biopsy of the reperfused zone shows hypercontracted sarcomeres with eosinophilic contraction bands and focal hemorrhage.

Sudden cardiac death
p. 7
Definition

Unexpected death from a cardiac cause, often due to fatal ventricular arrhythmia in ischemic disease.

Pattern

May be the first manifestation of coronary disease; structural heart disease, cardiomyopathy, and channel disorders broaden the differential.

Vignette

A previously functional 62-year-old man collapses without warning while jogging and is found in ventricular fibrillation. Postmortem examination shows severe coronary atherosclerosis but no large new area of necrosis.

2.04 Remodeling and clinical heart-failure phenotypes

7 cards · source p. 7
Acute decompensated heart failure
High yieldp. 7
Definition

Abrupt worsening of heart-failure symptoms and filling pressures, often from a precipitating stressor.

Pattern

Rapid dyspnea, orthopnea, pulmonary edema, raised JVP, edema, and possible hypoperfusion after ischemia, arrhythmia, infection, medication lapse, or renal decline.

Vignette

A patient with chronic HFrEF stops diuretics, gains weight, becomes orthopneic, and presents with hypoxemia, crackles, raised JVP, and leg edema.

Distinguish

Identify wet versus dry and warm versus cold physiology rather than assuming all dyspnea is volume overload.

Outcome

Respiratory failure, cardiogenic shock, renal injury, and arrhythmia.

HFpEF
High yieldp. 7
Definition

Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction from impaired relaxation and increased ventricular stiffness.

Pattern

Congestion occurs despite a preserved EF; long-standing hypertension, aging, obesity, and infiltrative disease are common contexts; S4 may be present.

Vignette

A 76-year-old woman with obesity and long-standing hypertension has exertional dyspnea, pulmonary congestion, concentric LV hypertrophy, and an ejection fraction of 65%. Doppler findings show impaired relaxation and elevated filling pressures.

Left versus right failure
High yieldp. 7
Definition

Left failure raises pulmonary venous pressure; right failure raises systemic venous pressure.

Pattern

Left: dyspnea, orthopnea, PND, crackles, pulmonary edema. Right: raised JVP, edema, ascites, congestive hepatomegaly. Either side can eventually burden the other.

Vignette

One patient presents with orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, and bibasilar crackles; another has elevated JVP, hepatomegaly, ascites, and dependent edema. The dominant venous compartment identifies which side is failing clinically.

Concentric hypertrophy
p. 7
Definition

Parallel sarcomere addition that thickens the ventricular wall in response to pressure overload.

Pattern

Hypertension or aortic stenosis produces reduced compliance and diastolic dysfunction before systolic failure.

Vignette

A patient with long-standing severe hypertension has a thick left-ventricular wall, a relatively small cavity, preserved ejection fraction, and an S4. Dyspnea occurs because the stiff ventricle fills poorly.

Eccentric hypertrophy
p. 7
Definition

Series sarcomere addition with chamber dilation in response to volume overload or myocardial loss.

Pattern

Regurgitant lesions, shunts, or dilated cardiomyopathy produce increased end-diastolic volume and later systolic dysfunction.

Vignette

A patient with years of severe aortic valve leakage has a markedly dilated left-ventricular cavity with increased myocardial mass and an S3. Progressive volume loading eventually reduces systolic function.

HFrEF
p. 7
Definition

Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction from impaired ventricular contraction.

Pattern

Dilated ventricle, low EF, S3, pulmonary/systemic congestion, and neurohormonal activation. Ischemia and dilated cardiomyopathy are common causes.

Vignette

A patient with a prior large anterior infarction has orthopnea, pulmonary crackles, an S3, displaced apical impulse, and an ejection fraction of 25%. Neurohormonal activation accompanies poor forward flow and congestion.

High-output heart failure
p. 7
Definition

Heart failure despite increased cardiac output because systemic demand or low vascular resistance exceeds cardiac capacity.

Pattern

Warm extremities and wide pulse pressure may accompany congestion in severe anemia, thyrotoxicosis, AV fistula, beriberi, or Paget disease.

Vignette

A severely anemic patient has tachycardia, warm extremities, bounding pulses, elevated output, pulmonary crackles, and edema.

Distinguish

Low-output failure produces cool hypoperfused extremities and reduced forward flow.

Outcome

Progressive chamber dilation, congestion, and eventual low-output failure.

2.05 Rhythm and conduction

Atrial fibrillation
High yieldp. 8
Definition

Disorganized atrial electrical activity causes loss of effective atrial contraction and an irregular ventricular response.

Pattern

Irregularly irregular rhythm, absent discrete P waves, palpitations or asymptomatic disease, and thromboembolic risk from atrial stasis.

Vignette

An older patient with hypertension has sudden palpitations and an irregularly irregular pulse; ECG shows no organized P waves.

Distinguish

Atrial flutter has organized sawtooth atrial activity and often a regular conduction ratio.

Outcome

Ischemic stroke, tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy, and heart failure.

Atrioventricular block
High yieldp. 8
Definition

Delayed or failed conduction of atrial impulses through the AV node or His-Purkinje system.

Pattern

First degree: prolonged PR. Mobitz I: progressive PR then dropped QRS. Mobitz II: fixed PR with sudden dropped QRS. Third degree: AV dissociation.

Vignette

A patient with an anterior MI develops sudden dropped QRS complexes without progressive PR lengthening and becomes lightheaded.

Distinguish

Mobitz I is commonly nodal and progressive; Mobitz II implies infranodal disease and greater progression risk.

Outcome

Syncope, severe bradycardia, progression to complete block, and sudden death.

Torsades de pointes
High yieldp. 8
Definition

Polymorphic ventricular tachycardia arising in the setting of prolonged ventricular repolarization.

Pattern

QRS complexes twist around the baseline after QT prolongation from drugs, congenital disease, bradycardia, low potassium, or low magnesium.

Vignette

A patient taking several QT-prolonging drugs becomes syncopal; ECG shows a prolonged QT followed by twisting polymorphic VT.

Distinguish

Ordinary monomorphic VT has a stable QRS morphology and is not defined by long QT.

Outcome

Degeneration to ventricular fibrillation and sudden death.

Ventricular fibrillation
High yieldp. 8
Definition

Chaotic ventricular electrical activity produces no coordinated contraction or effective cardiac output.

Pattern

Immediate collapse, absent pulse, and an irregular chaotic ECG without organized QRS complexes.

Vignette

A patient collapses after chest pain; monitor shows disorganized oscillations and no palpable pulse.

Distinguish

Pulseless VT remains organized and often regular; VF is chaotic.

Outcome

Sudden cardiac death without immediate defibrillation and CPR.

Ventricular tachycardia
High yieldp. 8
Definition

Three or more consecutive ventricular beats at a rapid rate, commonly generated by scar-related re-entry.

Pattern

Regular wide-complex tachycardia, AV dissociation, capture/fusion beats, and structural heart disease strongly favor VT.

Vignette

A patient with a prior MI develops palpitations, hypotension, and a regular wide-complex rhythm with AV dissociation.

Distinguish

Treat a wide-complex tachycardia as VT when uncertain, especially with structural disease.

Outcome

Ventricular fibrillation, syncope, cardiogenic shock, and sudden death.

Atrial flutter
p. 8
Definition

Macro-reentrant atrial tachyarrhythmia, usually circulating around the tricuspid annulus.

Pattern

Sawtooth flutter waves near 300/min with fixed or variable AV conduction; ventricular rate is often near 150 with 2:1 block.

Vignette

A patient has regular palpitations at 150/min; ECG shows repetitive inferior-lead sawtooth waves at twice the ventricular rate.

Distinguish

Atrial fibrillation has chaotic baseline activity and an irregularly irregular response.

Outcome

Thromboembolism, rapid ventricular response, and heart failure.

Re-entry and abnormal automaticity
p. 8
Definition

Re-entry requires two pathways with different conduction/refractory properties; abnormal automaticity reflects spontaneous impulse generation outside the normal pacemaker hierarchy.

Pattern

Re-entry produces abrupt stereotyped tachycardia; enhanced automaticity follows ischemia, electrolytes, adrenergic stimulation, or drug toxicity.

Vignette

A patient with a scarred ventricle has an abrupt monomorphic tachycardia, while another with digoxin toxicity develops multiple ectopic rhythms.

Distinguish

Triggered activity from early or delayed afterdepolarizations is mechanistically distinct but also produces ectopy/tachyarrhythmia.

Outcome

Sustained tachycardia, syncope, hemodynamic collapse, or sudden death.

Sinus bradycardia and sinus tachycardia
p. 8
Definition

Sinus rhythms originate in the SA node but are slower or faster than the expected resting rate.

Pattern

Normal P before each QRS with consistent PR; determine whether the rate is physiologic, medication-related, endocrine, hypoxic, infectious, or conduction-system disease.

Vignette

An athlete has an asymptomatic rate of 48 with normal P-QRS relationships; another patient with fever and dehydration has a gradual rate of 120.

Distinguish

Re-entry tachycardia starts and stops abruptly; sinus tachycardia usually changes gradually with the trigger.

Outcome

Symptomatic bradycardia may cause syncope; persistent tachycardia may worsen ischemia or heart failure.

Supraventricular re-entrant tachycardia
p. 8
Definition

A regular tachycardia generated above the ventricles, commonly by an AV-nodal or accessory-pathway re-entry circuit.

Pattern

Abrupt onset and termination, regular narrow QRS, rapid rate, and often hidden retrograde P waves; AV-node blockade may terminate an AV-node-dependent circuit.

Vignette

A young adult develops sudden regular palpitations at 190/min; the narrow-complex rhythm stops after an AV-nodal intervention.

Distinguish

Sinus tachycardia has gradual onset and a visible physiologic trigger; ventricular tachycardia is usually wide complex.

Outcome

Syncope, hypotension, ischemia, or tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy.

Endomyocardial fibrosis

A patient from a tropical region develops progressive ascites, edema, and severe diastolic dysfunction with apical endocardial thickening.

Hypersensitivity myocarditis

A patient develops rash, eosinophilia, and new ventricular dysfunction shortly after starting a medication; coronary testing is unrevealing.

Loeffler endomyocarditis

Mural thrombi, systemic emboli, restrictive failure, and valve regurgitation.

Myocarditis
p. 9

Pattern

Chest pain, arrhythmia, HF, or sudden death after an illness; lymphocytic injury is common in viral disease, eosinophils suggest hypersensitivity, and giant cells imply severe disease.

Vignette

A young adult develops chest pain, palpitations, elevated troponin, and new systolic dysfunction one week after a viral-like illness. Coronary arteries are unobstructed, and biopsy shows lymphocytes with adjacent myocyte injury.

Parasitic and fungal myocarditis

Direct infection of myocardium by parasites or fungi, especially with immunosuppression or specific exposure.

Pattern

Vignette

An immunocompromised patient with systemic infection develops new arrhythmia and ventricular dysfunction; myocardial tissue contains organisms with inflammation.

Distinguish

Arrhythmia, heart failure, disseminated infection, and death.

Restrictive cardiomyopathy
p. 9
Definition

Stiff ventricular myocardium limits filling while systolic function may initially be preserved.

Pattern

Vignette

An older adult with systemic amyloid develops edema, ascites, elevated JVP, biatrial enlargement, and severe diastolic dysfunction despite a near-normal ejection fraction and nondilated ventricles. ECG voltage is unexpectedly low relative to apparent wall thickness.

Viral lymphocytic myocarditis
p. 9
Definition

Viral-triggered immune myocardial injury characterized by lymphocytic inflammation and myocyte necrosis.

Pattern

Viral prodrome followed by chest pain, arrhythmia, troponin rise, new heart failure, or sudden death; may evolve into dilated cardiomyopathy.

Vignette

A young adult develops fatigue and dyspnea one week after a viral illness; troponin is elevated, coronary arteries are normal, and MRI shows patchy myocardial inflammation.

Malignant arrhythmia, cardiogenic shock, or chronic dilated cardiomyopathy.

2.07 Valve stenosis, regurgitation, and prolapse

Mitral regurgitation
High yieldp. 9
Definition

Pattern

Holosystolic apical murmur radiating to axilla; acute MR causes pulmonary edema, whereas chronic MR produces LA/LV dilation.

Vignette

Three days after an inferior coronary event, a patient abruptly develops severe dyspnea, pulmonary edema, and a new holosystolic apical murmur radiating to the axilla. Echocardiography shows systolic flow back into the left atrium.

Aortic regurgitation
p. 9
Definition

Diastolic backflow from aorta to LV due to leaflet disease or aortic-root dilation.

Pattern

Early diastolic decrescendo murmur, wide pulse pressure, bounding pulses, LV volume overload, and eccentric hypertrophy.

Vignette

A patient with a dilated aortic root has bounding pulses, a wide pulse pressure, and a high-pitched early diastolic decrescendo murmur along the left sternal border. Imaging shows chronic LV dilation from volume overload.

Aortic stenosis
p. 9
Definition

Obstruction of left-ventricular outflow, commonly from calcific degeneration or a bicuspid valve.

Pattern

Harsh crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur radiating to carotids, delayed weak pulse, and the triad of angina, syncope, and dyspnea. Causes concentric LV hypertrophy.

Vignette

An 82-year-old man has exertional syncope, angina, and dyspnea with a harsh crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur radiating to the carotids. Carotid upstrokes are weak and delayed, and the LV wall is concentrically thickened.

Mitral stenosis
p. 9
Definition

Obstruction of left-atrial emptying, most often a late consequence of rheumatic heart disease.

Pattern

Opening snap followed by diastolic rumble, LA enlargement, atrial fibrillation, pulmonary hypertension, and right-heart strain.

Vignette

A 46-year-old woman with remote rheumatic fever develops exertional dyspnea, hemoptysis, and atrial fibrillation. Auscultation reveals an opening snap followed by a low-pitched diastolic rumble at the apex.

Mitral valve prolapse
p. 9
Definition

Myxomatous redundant mitral leaflets billow into the left atrium during systole.

Pattern

Vignette

A thin young woman with palpitations has a midsystolic click followed by a late systolic murmur. The click moves earlier when she stands and later when she squats.

Prosthetic-valve dysfunction
p. 9
Definition

Pattern

Vignette

A patient with a mechanical valve has sudden dyspnea and muffled valve clicks after subtherapeutic anticoagulation; imaging shows impaired leaflet motion.

Distinguish

Separate thrombosis from pannus or degeneration with timing, anticoagulation history, and imaging.

Outcome

Embolism, acute obstruction, regurgitation, hemolysis, and heart failure.

Acute pericarditis
