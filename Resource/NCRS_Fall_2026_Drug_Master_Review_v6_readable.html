Core exam patterns

Nitrates + sildenafil/PDE5 inhibitor -> life-threatening hypotension.
ACEI / ARB / aliskiren -> contraindicated in pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis, hyperkalemia (slide framework).
Sacubitril/valsartan + ACEI or <36 h washout -> angioedema risk.
Clonidine abrupt withdrawal -> rebound hypertension.
Beta blocker abrupt withdrawal in CAD -> unstable angina / MI / death risk.
Ivabradine + verapamil/diltiazem -> CYP3A4-mediated bradycardia risk; avoid.
Hypokalemia increases digoxin binding/toxicity; severe toxicity -> digoxin immune Fab.
Class IC antiarrhythmics + structural heart disease/post-MI -> fatal ventricular proarrhythmia risk.
Heparin + PF4 IgG -> HIT: thrombocytopenia + thrombosis; stop heparin and use non-heparin anticoagulant.
Warfarin in pregnancy -> contraindicated; crosses placenta.
Clopidogrel requires CYP2C19 activation; omeprazole can reduce activation.
Lamotrigine/carbamazepine rash -> stop; SJS/TEN black-box pattern.
Tolcapone -> fulminant hepatic necrosis; entacapone preferred.
Volatile anesthetic + succinylcholine in RYR1 susceptibility -> malignant hyperthermia; dantrolene.
Bupivacaine -> most cardiotoxic local anesthetic; prilocaine -> methemoglobinemia.

1. DIURETICS

Start with nephron site -> intended effect -> electrolyte signature.
Loop diuretics
Furosemide | Torsemide | Bumetanide
WHEN
Acute pulmonary edema and heart failure volume overload
WHY
Block Na+/K+/2Cl- cotransport in the thick ascending limb
WATCH
Ototoxicity
KEY
Major exam pattern: strong diuresis + K+/Mg2+/Ca2+ loss.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • Acute pulmonary edema and heart failure volume overload
  • Hypertension when stronger diuresis is needed; advanced renal dysfunction
  • Hypercalcemia and hyperkalemia are slide-listed uses
Key action / why it works
  • Block Na+/K+/2Cl- cotransport in the thick ascending limb
  • Increase urine volume and urinary Na+, K+, Ca2+, Mg2+; lower volume/preload
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Ototoxicity
  • Hyperuricemia
  • Acute hypovolemia
  • K+ depletion and hypomagnesemia
  • Hypersensitivity
CORE
  • Major exam pattern: strong diuresis + K+/Mg2+/Ca2+ loss.
  • Loop diuretics are the most important intervention for acute decompensated HF with volume overload.
Source: All Slides PDF pp. 328-337, 962; official drug list p. 1
Thiazides
Chlorthalidone | Hydrochlorothiazide | Metolazone
WHEN
Preferred diuretic for mild-moderate hypertension with normal renal/cardiac function
WHY
Block Na+/Cl- cotransporter in distal tubule
WATCH
Hypersensitivity; sexual dysfunction
KEY
When GFR falls below about 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, the HTN slides recommend replacing thiazide with a loop.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • Preferred diuretic for mild-moderate hypertension with normal renal/cardiac function
  • Heart failure, hypercalciuria/kidney-stone prevention, diabetes insipidus, premenstrual edema
  • Thiazide/CCB are preferred first-line choices in Black and/or elderly patients in the slides
Key action / why it works
  • Block Na+/Cl- cotransporter in distal tubule
  • Increase Na+, K+, Mg2+ excretion; decrease urinary Ca2+
  • Chronic BP effect reflects lower peripheral vascular resistance
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Hypokalemia, hyponatremia, metabolic alkalosis
  • Hyperuricemia, hypercalcemia, hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia
  • Hypersensitivity; sexual dysfunction
CORE
  • When GFR falls below about 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, the HTN slides recommend replacing thiazide with a loop.
  • Metolazone remains potent in advanced kidney failure; chlorthalidone has a long half-life.
Source: All Slides PDF pp. 338-346, 461-462; official drug list p. 1

Spironolactone | Eplerenone
WHEN

WHY
Block aldosterone action in collecting tubule -> decrease Na+ retention and spare K+
WATCH
Hyperkalemia
KEY

Intended effect / clinical use
  • Hypertension and edema
  • Heart failure disease modification
  • Primary hyperaldosteronism - diagnosis/treatment context in slides
Key action / why it works
  • Block aldosterone action in collecting tubule -> decrease Na+ retention and spare K+
  • In HF, block aldosterone-associated Na+ retention and myocardial hypertrophy
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Hyperkalemia
  • GI upset / gastritis / peptic ulcer
  • Lethargy/confusion
  • Spironolactone endocrine effects: gynecomastia, decreased libido, menstrual irregularities
CORE
  • HF slide: discontinue/avoid MRA when eGFR <=30 mL/min/1.73 m2 or serum K+ >=5.0 mEq/L.
  • Hyperkalemia risk rises with ACEI/ARB/ARNI, K+ supplements, or renal failure.

2. CHRONIC ANTIHYPERTENSIVES

The discriminators are RAAS dependence, cardiac vs vascular selectivity, and the reflex responses.
ACE inhibitors
Captopril | Enalapril | Lisinopril
WHEN
First-line primary hypertension
WHY
Block Ang I -> Ang II conversion -> decrease vasoconstriction and aldosterone
WATCH
Hypotension
KEY
CONTRAINDICATED: pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis, hyperkalemia.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • First-line primary hypertension
  • Unilateral renal artery stenosis (renin-dependent HTN)
  • Diabetes/CKD with nephropathy or albuminuria
  • Heart failure
  • Standard-of-care post-MI context: reduce deleterious remodeling
Key action / why it works
  • Block Ang I -> Ang II conversion -> decrease vasoconstriction and aldosterone
  • Block bradykinin degradation -> additional vasodilation
  • Dilate efferent arteriole -> small reversible GFR decrease / creatinine rise can occur
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Hypotension
  • Hyperkalemia
  • Dry hacking cough
  • Angioedema
  • Small creatinine rise; rash/taste change/drug fever
CORE
  • CONTRAINDICATED: pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis, hyperkalemia.
  • Bilateral renal artery stenosis: removing Ang II efferent constriction can abruptly drop GFR.
Source: All Slides PDF pp. 463-465; official drug list p. 1
Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs)
Losartan | Valsartan
WHEN
Hypertension and heart failure similar to ACEI
WHY
Direct AT1 blockade -> decrease Ang II vasoconstriction and aldosterone
WATCH
Hypotension
KEY
CONTRAINDICATED: pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis, hyperkalemia.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • Hypertension and heart failure similar to ACEI
  • Diabetic nephropathy
  • Good ACEI alternative when bradykinin-mediated cough occurs
Key action / why it works
  • Direct AT1 blockade -> decrease Ang II vasoconstriction and aldosterone
  • Do NOT inhibit bradykinin breakdown
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Hypotension
  • Hyperkalemia
  • Rare acute renal failure
  • Angioedema less likely than ACEI
  • Losartan inhibits URAT1 -> lowers serum uric acid
CORE
  • CONTRAINDICATED: pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis, hyperkalemia.
  • ACEI + ARB combination is not recommended in the slides because kidney function may worsen.
Source: All Slides PDF pp. 465-466; official drug list p. 1

Beta blockers in hypertension
Propranolol | Metoprolol | Atenolol | Pindolol
WHEN
First-line only when compelling indication: CAD, heart failure, or post-MI
WHY
Decrease HR, contractility and cardiac output via beta1 block
WATCH
Bradycardia, reduced exercise capacity, HF, hypotension, AV block
KEY
DO NOT STOP ABRUPTLY: slides warn of unstable angina, MI, or death in CAD.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • First-line only when compelling indication: CAD, heart failure, or post-MI
  • Otherwise add-on/second-line in primary prevention
Key action / why it works
  • Decrease HR, contractility and cardiac output via beta1 block
  • Decrease renin release from JG cells
  • Propranolol = nonselective beta1/beta2; metoprolol/atenolol = beta1 selective; pindolol = partial agonist/ISA
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Bradycardia, reduced exercise capacity, HF, hypotension, AV block
  • Fatigue/sexual effects
  • Can mask tachycardic warning of hypoglycemia
  • Nonselective agents can bronchoconstrict
CORE
  • DO NOT STOP ABRUPTLY: slides warn of unstable angina, MI, or death in CAD.
  • Avoid nonselective propranolol in asthma/COPD; avoid beta blockade in sinus bradycardia/partial AV block.
Source: All Slides PDF pp. 473-475; official drug list p. 1

Alpha1 blockers
Prazosin | Doxazosin
WHEN
Alternative/add-on hypertension therapy
WHY
Block alpha1 receptors in arterioles and venules -> lower PVR/BP
WATCH
First-dose orthostatic hypotension with dizziness/palpitations/syncope
KEY
Give first dose small and at bedtime because of the first-dose phenomenon.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • Alternative/add-on hypertension therapy
  • Unique useful situation emphasized: men with hypertension + BPH

  • Block alpha1 receptors in arterioles and venules -> lower PVR/BP
  • Less reflex tachycardia than nonselective alpha blockade because presynaptic alpha2 feedback remains
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • First-dose orthostatic hypotension with dizziness/palpitations/syncope
  • Na+ and water retention
  • Dizziness, drowsiness, headache, low energy, nausea
CORE
  • Give first dose small and at bedtime because of the first-dose phenomenon.
  • Doxazosin monotherapy increased CHF risk in the slide discussion.

Non-dihydropyridine CCBs
Verapamil | Diltiazem
WHEN
Hypertension, angina, and arrhythmias
WHY
L-type Ca2+ channel block in myocardium/nodal tissue + vessels -> lower HR, conduction, contractility and PVR
WATCH
Hypotension/peripheral edema
KEY
RELATIVELY CONTRAINDICATED with beta blockers, 2nd/3rd degree AV block, or severe LV systolic dysfunction.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • Hypertension, angina, and arrhythmias
  • Verapamil has greatest myocardial depressant effect; diltiazem is intermediate between verapamil and DHPs
Key action / why it works
  • L-type Ca2+ channel block in myocardium/nodal tissue + vessels -> lower HR, conduction, contractility and PVR
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Bradycardia, AV block, heart failure
  • Hypotension/peripheral edema
  • Verapamil: constipation (~7%) and gingival hyperplasia
CORE
  • RELATIVELY CONTRAINDICATED with beta blockers, 2nd/3rd degree AV block, or severe LV systolic dysfunction.
  • Do not use as if they were DHPs: these directly depress the heart.
Source: All Slides PDF pp. 468-470, 452-453; official drug list p. 1
Central alpha2 agonists
Clonidine | Methyldopa
WHEN
Chronic HTN / hypertensive-crisis contexts but not first-line due CNS side effects
WHY
Stimulate central alpha2 receptors -> reduce sympathetic outflow and increase vagal tone -> lower HR/CO/PVR/renin
WATCH
Orthostatic hypotension, dizziness, CNS/anticholinergic effects
KEY
CLONIDINE ABRUPT WITHDRAWAL -> rebound hypertension.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • Chronic HTN / hypertensive-crisis contexts but not first-line due CNS side effects
  • Methyldopa = drug of choice for pregnancy-induced hypertension in the slides
Key action / why it works
  • Stimulate central alpha2 receptors -> reduce sympathetic outflow and increase vagal tone -> lower HR/CO/PVR/renin
  • Methyldopa is converted to alpha-methylnorepinephrine
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Orthostatic hypotension, dizziness, CNS/anticholinergic effects
  • Na+ and water retention
  • Methyldopa: Coombs-positive hemolytic anemia, hepatitis, reversible lupus-like syndrome
CORE
  • CLONIDINE ABRUPT WITHDRAWAL -> rebound hypertension.
  • Persistent transaminase elevation with methyldopa -> discontinue per slide.
Source: All Slides PDF pp. 475-476; official drug list p. 1
Direct arteriolar vasodilators
Hydralazine | Minoxidil
WHEN
Not first-line; generally later-line chronic HTN
WHY
Both are arterial vasodilators
WATCH
Hydralazine: hypotension, reflex tachycardia, fluid retention, reversible lupus-like syndrome, headache/nausea/flushing
KEY
Hydralazine lupus is more common in slow acetylators.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • Not first-line; generally later-line chronic HTN
  • Hydralazine: moderate-severe HTN, HF, and severe pregnancy-related HTN/preeclampsia-eclampsia context
  • Minoxidil: severe/malignant refractory HTN; topical use for androgenetic alopecia
Key action / why it works
  • Both are arterial vasodilators
  • Minoxidil opens K+ channels and hyperpolarizes vascular smooth muscle
  • Vasodilation triggers reflex tachycardia + renin -> Na+/water retention
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Hydralazine: hypotension, reflex tachycardia, fluid retention, reversible lupus-like syndrome, headache/nausea/flushing
  • Minoxidil: severe reflex tachycardia/fluid retention; hypertrichosis
CORE
  • Hydralazine lupus is more common in slow acetylators.
  • Minoxidil is usually paired with loop diuretic + beta blocker; hydralazine with diuretic + beta blocker to blunt reflex effects.
Source: All Slides PDF pp. 471-472, 550-554; official drug list p. 1

3. HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCIES + PULMONARY HTN

Emergency rule: short-acting, titratable IV therapy; drug choice depends on the organ problem.
Hypertensive-emergency treatment rule

WHY
Progressive rather than abrupt reduction
WATCH

KEY

Know induction vs maintenance, inhaled-agent kinetics, and the signature toxicities.

Inhaled + IV agents + adjuncts

General anesthesia aims for analgesia, amnesia, unconsciousness, reflex suppression, and skeletal-muscle relaxation

15. LOCAL ANESTHETICS

Same Na+ channel target; high-yield differences are ester vs amide metabolism and signature toxicities.
Local anesthetic core mechanism
Cocaine | Procaine | Tetracaine | Benzocaine | Lidocaine | Bupivacaine | Prilocaine | Ropivacaine
WHEN
Reversibly block sensory nerve conduction without unconsciousness
WHY
Block voltage-gated Na+ channels from the intracellular side; active/open/inactivated channels have higher affinity -> use/time dependence
WATCH
Systemic toxicity begins with CNS stimulation/tremor -> convulsions, followed by CNS depression/respiratory failure
KEY
Infected acidic tissue -> less nonionized drug -> poorer membrane penetration -> local anesthetic works less effectively.
Intended effect / clinical use
  • Reversibly block sensory nerve conduction without unconsciousness
Key action / why it works
  • Block voltage-gated Na+ channels from the intracellular side; active/open/inactivated channels have higher affinity -> use/time dependence
  • Uncharged form crosses membrane; cationic form is most active at receptor
Adverse effects / trade-offs
  • Systemic toxicity begins with CNS stimulation/tremor -> convulsions, followed by CNS depression/respiratory failure
  • Cardiac Na+ blockade depresses pacemaker activity/excitability/conduction
CORE
  • Infected acidic tissue -> less nonionized drug -> poorer membrane penetration -> local anesthetic works less effectively.
